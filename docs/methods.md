# Methods for the frozen computational experiments

The common task is the token sequence `(0,1,2,0,3,1,2,3)`. Each natural probe starts from zero hidden state and predicts the next token at seven teacher-forced transitions. Raw cross-entropy uses natural logarithms and the sequence score is the equal mean of those seven transition losses. Hidden trajectories and centered output logits are recorded. For each transition, the task-space reference is the mean of the corresponding A and B centered-logit states. Absolute Euclidean distance to that reference, A/B separation, and distance divided by separation plus the fixed epsilon are separate measures; normalization happens before transition averaging.

The canonical cohort contains 20 paired High/Low monotonic-objective seeds at registered update 170. The constructive cohort contains 20 matched seeds for each historical C0 sequence-only, C1 contrast-only, C2 closure-only, and C3 full condition. C0 is not the High monotonic cohort. Genuine acquisition curves come from pre-update training logs; final checkpoint replay is used only for final-state evaluation. A2 matches High update 170 to registered Low updates 0–170 within seed using the same incoming context and separate A/B CE calipers of 0.05 nat. This rule was fixed on familiar data before new-cue evaluation. One pair is supported, and the Low intermediate parameter state is not saved, so no matched new-cue effect is estimated.

The frozen cue registry defines A, B, Mixed, NoContext, and four graded Novel directions. Novel30Mean and Novel60Mean each average two directions within seed; NovelMean averages all four directions and is not averaged again with its constituents. Historical Novel1–4 are reported as LegacyNovel1–4. The physical third input channel is zero for the new cues and inactive in these historical models. No cue was selected or altered using model outcomes.

A3 constructs one target at each transition from the corresponding familiar A/B hidden midpoint. Repeated replacement estimates a sufficiency ceiling; acute placement covers every one of seven positions. Natural, wrong-position, norm-matched orthogonal, and familiar endpoint placements remain separate controls. A4 places the midpoint once and observes subsequent lag under native (D0), zero-cue (D1), or physical Mixed (D2) continuation. Lag-zero rescue, remaining-sequence rescue including the placement transition, and post-injection effects are distinct. At lag `l`, `7−l` positions are available; position averaging occurs within seed before seed inference. A5 branches from identical prestates into native, A, B, Mixed, and zero physical cues without hidden replacement, then tests one-step pulse and persistent cue takeover from natural prefixes.

A6 scores natural behavior and familiar separation in C0–C3. C2/C3 historically used familiar-derived neutral closure at physical Mixed during training; this is disclosed as representational supervision, not protected new-Novel behavioral supervision. A7 pairs C3−C0 within seed in absolute task distance and raw CE, with normalized residual as sensitivity. A8 computes the effective contrast coordinate and discarded cue component, and verifies exact predicted behavioral aliases. No new pathway or model is introduced to explain an unfavorable result.

All complete-cohort inferential summaries use a shared 10,000 × 20 seed-resampling matrix from RNG seed 20260925. Paired condition differences are taken within seed before resampling, with percentile 95% intervals. Transition, direction, position, and lag rows are never treated as independent networks. The report does not substitute p-value thresholds for effect sizes or use significance to choose panels. Human analyses are outside this computational freeze.
